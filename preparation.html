👩‍🍳 Step-by-Step Guide

How to Prepare

All five Mom's Mix premixes follow a simple batter-and-ferment process — preserving the authentic taste of traditional South Indian cooking.

💡

Before You Start

All measurements use a standard cup or the same vessel as your "1 measure." Keep it consistent throughout. Ferment in a warm spot — ideally 25–30°C — for best results. The batter should roughly double in volume during fermentation.

🍚

Idli Premix

Soft, fluffy steamed rice cakes — the ultimate South Indian breakfast comfort.

Mix : Water  1 : 1¼
Ferment  10–12 hrs
🌿 No Preservatives ⏱ Ready in 15 min after ferment 🍽 Serves 4–6
1

Take 1 measure of Mom's Mix Idli Mix into a mixing bowl.

2

Add 1¼ measures of water gradually and mix well to form a lump-free batter.

3

Add salt to taste and stir until evenly combined into a smooth batter.

4

Cover loosely and allow the batter to ferment overnight or for 10–12 hours in a warm place.

5

Grease idli moulds lightly with oil. Pour batter into each mould and steam for 10–12 minutes until a skewer inserted comes out clean.

6

Allow to cool for 2 minutes, then carefully remove from moulds using a wet spoon. Serve hot with sambar and coconut chutney.

💡 Tip: If the idlis are too dense, the batter may not have fermented enough. Place in a slightly warm oven (light on) to encourage fermentation on cooler days.

Dosa Premix

Thin, crispy, golden crepes — the most iconic South Indian breakfast.

Mix : Water  1 : 1½
Ferment  10–12 hrs
🌿 No Preservatives ⏱ Ready in 20 min after ferment 🍽 Serves 4–6
1

Take 1 measure of Mom's Mix Dosa Mix into a mixing bowl.

2

Add 1½ measures of water gradually, mixing to form a smooth, pourable batter (slightly thinner than idli batter).

3

Add salt to taste and stir evenly.

4

Cover loosely and allow to ferment overnight or for 10–12 hours.

5

Heat a tawa on medium-high. Splash a little water — it should evaporate instantly. Wipe with a half-cut onion dipped in oil.

6

Pour a ladleful of batter and spread in a thin circular motion from the centre outward. Drizzle oil along the edges. Cook until the base is golden and crisp, then fold and serve.

💡 Tip: A cast-iron tawa gives the crispiest dosas. Keep the heat on medium-high and do not flip — a well-made dosa only cooks on one side.

Dibba Rotte Premix

Traditional Telugu thick pan cake — crispy outside, soft and spongy inside.

Mix : Water  1 : 1
Ferment  10–12 hrs
🌿 No Preservatives ⏱ Ready in 20–25 min after ferment 🍽 Serves 3–5
1

Take 1 measure of Mom's Mix Dibba Rotte Mix into a mixing bowl.

2

Add 1 measure of water and mix to form a thick, smooth batter.

3

Add salt to taste and stir well.

4

Allow the batter to ferment overnight or for 10–12 hours. The batter will rise and become slightly airy.

5

Heat a heavy-bottomed pan and add a generous drizzle of oil or ghee. Pour a thick layer of batter (about 1.5–2 cm thick). Cover with a lid.

6

Cook on medium-low heat until the bottom is deeply golden and the top surface appears dry and set. Flip carefully and cook the other side until crisp. Serve with pickle and yoghurt.

💡 Tip: Dibba Rotte is thicker than a dosa — patience is key. Cook low and slow. Adding a little ghee before flipping gives a beautiful golden crust.

Punugulu Premix

Andhra-style street-food fritters. Also makes Gunta Pongannalu & Uttapam.

Mix : Water  1 : 1
No fermentation needed
🌿 No Preservatives ⚡ No overnight wait 🍽 Makes 20–24 pieces
1

Take 1 measure of Mom's Mix Punugulu Mix into a mixing bowl.

2

Add 1 measure of water and mix into a thick, smooth batter.

3

Add salt to taste.

4

Add finely chopped onion and green chillies to taste and mix well.

5

Heat oil in a deep pan to medium-high. Drop spoonfuls of batter carefully into the oil. Fry until golden brown and crispy on all sides, turning as needed.

6

Drain on paper towel. Serve hot with coconut chutney or tomato chutney.

Also Makes — Same Batter, Different Dishes

🍳 Gunta Pongannalu

  • Add a little extra water to thin the batter slightly.
  • Heat a pongannalu / appe pan and brush with oil.
  • Fill each cavity ¾ full and cook covered on medium heat.
  • Flip once the bottom is set and cook until golden.

🥞 Uttapam

  • Add water to create a thicker, pourable batter.
  • Pour onto a hot tawa and spread gently — do not make it too thin.
  • Top with onion, tomato, green chilli or coriander.
  • Drizzle oil, cook covered until set, then flip to brown the top.
💡 Tip: For crispier punugulu, make sure the oil is hot enough before adding the batter. A small drop should sizzle and rise immediately.

Gare Premix

Savory lentil fritters — a South Indian festive classic and everyday delight.

Mix : Water  1 : 1
Rest  2 hrs
🌿 No Preservatives ⭐ Festive Favourite 🍽 Makes 15–20 pieces
1

Mix 1 measure of Mom's Mix Gare Mix with 1 measure of water. Stir to combine into a thick batter.

2

Add salt, grated ginger, chopped green chilli, finely chopped onions, and fresh coriander leaves. Mix well. (These fresh aromatics are not included in the pack — add to your taste.)

3

Allow the batter to rest for 2 hours before shaping. No overnight fermentation required.

4

Heat oil in a deep pan on medium heat. Wet your hands to prevent sticking. Take a portion of batter, shape into a flat disc, and make a hole in the centre.

5

Gently slide the shaped gare into the hot oil. Fry on medium heat, turning once, until both sides are golden brown and crispy.

6

Drain on paper towel. Serve hot with coconut chutney and sambar.

💡 Tip: Gare batter should be thick enough to hold its shape when you try to form a ring. If it feels too loose, let it rest a further 20–30 minutes. Wet hands make shaping much easier.
